Design System Launch Film – Blend
Context –
Blend is Juspay's design system—built to bring consistency and speed across products. The challenge was to create a launch film that reflects not just the visuals, but the behavior of the system.
https://blend.juspay.design/
Real Challenge
Getting alignment on what the video should be.
There were multiple directions: a slower, explainer-style video a more product-focused direction around Tokenizer
Neither fully captured what Blend stood for. A design system isn't something you explain—it's something you experience.
What made this tricky
We weren't just designing motion—we were aligning perspectives. Midway, we also realised something important: If motion is the core of the storytelling, we can't depend on externally created visuals. To make match cuts, rhythm, and transitions feel intentional, motion and visuals had to be designed together.
Iterations
The first version looked polished, but lacked flow—it showed the system without really feeling like one. That's when we shifted approach.

We took ownership of both visuals and motion, building them together instead of separately. This allowed us to design for match cuts, rhythm, and continuity from the ground up—making the film feel cohesive and fast.

We explored a third direction based on stakeholder feedback, bringing in more focus on Tokenizer. While valid, it pulled the narrative away from the design system. We resolved this by separating the two—keeping this film focused on Blend, and treating Tokenizer as a standalone story.
My Role
Motion design, concept exploration, and shaping both visual + motion direction across iterations.

Juspay Website — Motion Case Study
Context –
Redesigned the Juspay website to position the brand for a global audience. The shift wasn't just visual—it involved rethinking tone, depth, and how the product is presented.
https://juspay.io/br
The Challenge
Creating motion that feels sleek and premium, while still being lightweight and production-ready.
What made this tricky
This wasn't just about designing animations—it was about making them work at scale.
• Multiple Lotties on a single page affecting load performance
• Need for consistency across a new dark visual system
• Translating motion into dev-friendly, performant formats
Approach –
Defined a clear motion direction:
— subtle, soft, and premium
— no exaggerated or overly playful movement
Designed Lotties as a system—ensuring consistency across pages while keeping animations lightweight.
Worked closely with developers to solve performance issues:
• Removed non-essential animations
• Used WebM/video where needed
• Transitioned to Lottie format to reduce file size without compromising quality
Also took ownership of aligning motion across teams and driving implementation forward.
My Role
Motion design, defining motion principles, Lottie creation, cross-team alignment, and developer collaboration

Visa – Motion Case Study
Context –
Southeast Asia runs on QR payments—but each country operates on its own standard (QRIS, PromptPay, NETS). These systems don't talk to each other, making cross-border payments fragmented.
Visa's goal was to enable a single, seamless payment experience across countries, embedded inside existing wallet apps.
The Challenge –
Design motion for a system that is:
• invisible infrastructure
• used inside multiple third-party wallets
• expected to feel instantly understandable in unfamiliar contexts
The animations had to communicate "how this works" in seconds, without relying on text or explanation.
What made this tricky –
This wasn't about complex animation—it was about clarity and constraints.
• Aligning motion across multiple stakeholders
• Adapting to different design systems without breaking their visual language
• Communicating new payment flows (scan / tap) through minimal motion
• Handling a strong push for 3D visuals—which Lottie doesn't support directly
Approach –
Focused on clarity-first motion:
→ simple, readable, and instructional
→ designed for quick understanding at a glance
Defined motion patterns for:
• tap-to-pay
• scan-to-pay
• Visa branding moments
For the 3D requirement:
• Adapted fake 3D visuals within Lottie constraints
• balanced depth with file size and performance
• avoided heavy assets while still achieving the intended visual feel
My Role
Lottie creation, motion alignment across teams, and translating product flows into clear animation.
